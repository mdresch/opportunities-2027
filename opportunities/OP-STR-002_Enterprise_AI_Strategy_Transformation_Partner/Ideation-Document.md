1. Executive Summary

1.1 Project Overview

The Enterprise AI Strategy & Transformation Partner initiative is a strategic, end-to-end program designed to embed artificial intelligence (AI) into the core operations and decision-making processes of the organization. This project spans 12 weeks and focuses on aligning the Board of Directors and senior management around a unified AI vision, translating strategic ambition into actionable use cases, and establishing a dedicated corporate AI department to drive implementation. The goal is to create measurable value through AI-driven improvements in asset management, process productivity, project timeliness, and cost optimization.

This initiative is not merely a technological upgrade but a strategic transformation that redefines how the organization leverages data, automation, and intelligent systems to achieve competitive advantage. By adopting a PMBOK 7 framework, the project ensures a structured, value-driven approach that balances innovation with governance, risk management, and stakeholder alignment. The project’s success hinges on its ability to deliver both quick wins (to build momentum) and long-term investments (to sustain growth), while fostering a culture of AI adoption across the enterprise.

2. Project Charter

2.1 Purpose

The Enterprise AI Strategy & Transformation Partner project is initiated to embed AI into the organization’s strategic fabric, ensuring that AI is not just a tool but a core driver of value creation. The project’s purpose is threefold:

1. Strategic Alignment: Align the Board of Directors, senior management, and key stakeholders around a shared AI vision and roadmap.

2. Value Creation: Translate AI ambition into measurable outcomes through a prioritized portfolio of use cases.

3. Operational Excellence: Establish the infrastructure, governance, and talent required to sustain AI-driven transformation.

This project is critical because it addresses the gap between AI potential and organizational readiness. Without a structured approach, AI initiatives risk becoming siloed, underfunded, or misaligned with business priorities. By acting as an end-to-end partner, this project ensures that AI is strategically relevant, operationally feasible, and culturally embraced.

4. Implementation Plan

4.1 Key Components

4.1.1 Strategic Alignment

The Strategic Alignment component ensures that the Board of Directors and senior management are aligned around a shared AI vision. Key activities include:

1. Alignment Workshops: Facilitated sessions to agree on AI first principles, strategic direction, and investment thresholds.

2. Strategic Compass Development: A Board-approved document outlining AI ambition, KPIs, and governance framework.

3. Stakeholder Engagement: Regular briefings and workshops to maintain alignment and address concerns.

4.1.2 AI Use Case Portfolio

The AI Use Case Portfolio component focuses on prioritizing and developing AI use cases that balance risk, return, and time-to-impact. Key activities include:

1. Use Case Prioritization: Workshops to assess strategic contribution, value creation, and feasibility.

2. Portfolio Development: A balanced portfolio of 10-15 use cases, including 3-5 quick wins.

3. Feasibility Studies: Assessments of data availability, technical requirements, and implementation risks.

4.1.3 Corporate AI Department

The Corporate AI Department component establishes the infrastructure and talent required to deliver the AI use case portfolio. Key activities include:

1. Department Structure: Define roles, responsibilities, and governance framework.

2. Recruitment and Onboarding: Hire and train 10 FTEs to staff the AI department.

3. Performance Monitoring: Develop a dashboard to track KPIs and value realization.

4.1.4 Change Management

The Change Management component ensures that senior management and employees are mobilized around AI adoption. Key activities include:

1. Training Programs: Develop and deliver AI awareness training for senior management and employees.

2. Communication Campaigns: Quarterly town halls and newsletters to share progress and success stories.

3. Feedback Sessions: Gather input from stakeholders to address concerns and refine the approach.

2. Problem Statement

2.1 Current State and Enterprise Limitations

The organization currently operates in a fragmented AI landscape, where AI initiatives are pursued in isolation without a unified strategy or governance framework. This lack of cohesion manifests in several critical limitations:

1. Siloed AI Initiatives:

AI projects are developed and deployed within individual business units or departments, leading to redundant efforts, inconsistent standards, and limited scalability.

There is no centralized oversight to ensure alignment with strategic objectives or to prioritize high-impact use cases.

2. Lack of Strategic Alignment:

The Board of Directors and senior management have not reached consensus on the role of AI in the organization’s long-term strategy. This misalignment results in conflicting priorities, unclear investment thresholds, and missed opportunities for cross-functional collaboration.

Without a strategic compass, AI initiatives are often reactive rather than proactive, focusing on short-term gains rather than sustainable value creation.

3. Insufficient Governance and Risk Management:

AI deployments lack standardized governance frameworks, increasing the risk of compliance violations, data breaches, and ethical concerns.

There is no dedicated AI risk management process, leaving the organization vulnerable to operational, financial, and reputational risks.

4. Limited Talent and Resources:

The organization lacks a dedicated AI team to drive innovation and implementation. Existing AI talent is scattered across departments, leading to inefficient resource allocation and knowledge gaps.

There is no clear career path for AI professionals, making it difficult to attract and retain top talent.

5. Cultural Resistance to Change:

Employees and middle management exhibit skepticism and resistance to AI adoption due to fear of job displacement, lack of understanding, and perceived complexity.

There is no change management strategy to address these concerns, resulting in low engagement and slow adoption rates.

6. Technical Debt and Integration Challenges:

Existing IT infrastructure is not optimized for AI, with legacy systems that lack the scalability, interoperability, and data quality required for AI-driven solutions.

AI tools and platforms are not integrated with core business systems, leading to data silos, inefficiencies, and suboptimal performance.

These limitations collectively hinder the organization’s ability to leverage AI as a strategic enabler, resulting in missed opportunities, inefficiencies, and increased risks.
