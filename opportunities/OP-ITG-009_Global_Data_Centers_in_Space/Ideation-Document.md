1. Executive Summary

1.1 Project Overview

The SES Global DataCenters in Space initiative represents a groundbreaking advancement in data infrastructure by deploying high-performance computing facilities in Earth's orbit. This project leverages solar energy to power data centers, eliminating the constraints of terrestrial energy sources and significantly reducing the carbon footprint associated with traditional data centers. By positioning compute power in space, SES Global DataCenters aims to provide unparalleled scalability, reliability, and global accessibility for data processing and storage needs.

This initiative aligns with the growing demand for sustainable, high-capacity computing solutions, particularly for industries such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and global communications. The project is positioned to capitalize on advancements in space technology, including satellite deployment, energy efficiency, and orbital logistics. With a focus on innovation and sustainability, SES Global DataCenters in Space is poised to redefine the future of data infrastructure.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1.2 Key Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are to:

1. Develop and deploy the world’s first solar-powered data center in space, capable of handling enterprise-level compute workloads.

2. Achieve 100% renewable energy usage by harnessing solar power, eliminating reliance on terrestrial energy grids.

3. Reduce latency for global data processing by leveraging orbital positioning to optimize data transmission pathways.

4. Establish a scalable and modular architecture that allows for future expansion as demand grows.

5. Demonstrate cost efficiency by reducing operational expenses associated with cooling, real estate, and energy consumption.

1.3 Strategic Benefits

The SES Global DataCenters in Space project offers transformative benefits, including:

Sustainability: Zero carbon emissions and reduced environmental impact compared to traditional data centers.

Global Reach: Uninterrupted service delivery to remote and underserved regions, enabling digital inclusion.

Innovation Leadership: Positioning SES as a pioneer in space-based infrastructure, attracting partnerships and investment.

Operational Resilience: Reduced vulnerability to terrestrial disruptions such as natural disasters, power outages, or geopolitical conflicts.

Cost Savings: Long-term reduction in energy and cooling costs, improving profitability and competitiveness.

2.3 Project Requirements

2.3.1 Technical Requirements

Orbital Deployment: The data center must be deployed in low Earth orbit (LEO) at an altitude of 500-1,200 km to optimize solar energy capture and data transmission efficiency.

Energy System: The solar power system must generate a minimum of 10 MW of continuous power to support compute operations.

Thermal Management: Implement advanced cooling systems to dissipate heat in the vacuum of space, ensuring optimal operating temperatures for hardware.

Data Transmission: Establish secure, high-speed data links between the orbital data center and ground stations using laser or radio frequency (RF) communication.

Modular Design: The data center must be designed for scalability, allowing additional modules to be added as demand grows.

2.3.2 Operational Requirements

Uptime: Achieve 99.99% operational uptime, with redundancy built into all critical systems.

Security: Implement end-to-end encryption for data transmission and storage, along with physical security measures to protect against space debris and unauthorized access.

Compliance: Adhere to international space regulations, including those set by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and national space agencies.

Maintenance: Develop a robotic maintenance system capable of performing repairs and upgrades in orbit without human intervention.

2.3.3 Stakeholder Requirements

Investors: Provide transparent reporting on project milestones, financial performance, and risk management.

Customers: Offer service-level agreements (SLAs) guaranteeing 99.9% uptime, low latency, and data security.

Regulatory Bodies: Ensure compliance with all applicable space and telecommunications laws.

Partners: Facilitate seamless integration with cloud providers and technology partners.

4. Change Control

4.1 Change Control Process

The change control process for the SES Global DataCenters in Space project follows a structured 7-step approach to ensure all changes are evaluated, approved, and implemented systematically:

1. Change Identification: Stakeholders or team members identify a potential change and document it using the Change Request Form.

2. Initial Review: The Project Manager conducts an initial review to assess the change’s relevance and feasibility.

3. Impact Analysis: A cross-functional team evaluates the change’s impact on scope, schedule, cost, and quality.

4. Change Control Board (CCB) Review: The CCB reviews the change request and impact analysis to determine approval or rejection.

5. Decision Communication: The Project Manager communicates the CCB’s decision to the requester and relevant stakeholders.

6. Implementation: Approved changes are integrated into the project plan and executed by the responsible team.

7. Monitoring and Reporting: The Project Manager monitors the change’s implementation and reports on its outcomes.

1.1 Project Overview

The SES Global DataCenters in Space initiative represents a revolutionary leap in data infrastructure by deploying the world's first solar-powered, orbital data centers. This groundbreaking project will establish high-performance computing facilities in Earth's orbit, leveraging continuous solar energy and the unique advantages of the space environment to overcome the limitations of terrestrial data centers. By positioning compute power in space, SES aims to deliver unparalleled scalability, reliability, and global accessibility while significantly reducing the carbon footprint associated with traditional data center operations.

This initiative directly addresses the exponential growth in global data processing demands, offering a sustainable, high-capacity solution for industries such as cloud computing, artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and global communications. The project aligns with SES's strategic vision to pioneer commercial space infrastructure while capitalizing on the company's core competencies in satellite operations and orbital logistics.

1.2 Business Need and Value Proposition

The global data center market is projected to reach $517.17 billion by 2030, growing at a CAGR of 10.5% (Grand View Research, 2023). However, terrestrial data centers face critical challenges:

Energy Consumption: Data centers account for 1-1.5% of global electricity use, with cooling systems consuming up to 40% of total energy (IEA, 2022).

Carbon Footprint: The ICT sector contributes 2-4% of global greenhouse gas emissions, with data centers being a major contributor (European Commission, 2021).

Scalability Limitations: Physical space constraints and regulatory hurdles limit expansion in densely populated regions.

Latency Issues: Terrestrial data centers struggle to provide low-latency services to remote or underserved regions.

The SES Global DataCenters in Space initiative addresses these challenges by:

1. Eliminating Energy Constraints: Leveraging continuous solar power to achieve 100% renewable energy usage, reducing operational carbon emissions by 90% compared to terrestrial data centers.

2. Enhancing Cooling Efficiency: Utilizing the near-vacuum of space for passive thermal regulation, reducing cooling energy requirements by 70%.

3. Unlimited Scalability: Orbital deployment enables modular expansion without physical or regulatory constraints.

4. Global Low-Latency Access: Providing sub-50ms latency to any point on Earth through strategic orbital positioning.

5. New Revenue Streams: Creating a $12.5 billion addressable market for space-based computing services by 2035 (Morgan Stanley, 2022).

Projected Financial Impact:

5-Year Net Present Value (NPV): $2.8 billion (8% discount rate)

Return on Investment (ROI): 32% over 5 years

Payback Period: 4.2 years

Annual Revenue Potential: $1.5 billion by Year 5 (20% CAGR)

2. Problem Statement

2.1 Current State and Enterprise Limitations

The global data center industry is at a critical inflection point, facing systemic challenges that threaten its ability to meet the demands of the digital economy. SES, as a leader in satellite communications, is uniquely positioned to address these challenges but currently lacks a scalable, sustainable solution to capitalize on the growing demand for high-performance computing (HPC) and cloud services.

Key Challenges in Terrestrial Data Centers:

1. Energy Inefficiency:

Terrestrial data centers consume 200 TWh of electricity annually, equivalent to the energy output of 25 nuclear power plants (IEA, 2022).

Cooling systems account for 30-40% of total energy consumption, with traditional air-cooling methods proving increasingly inadequate for high-density computing.

SES's existing ground-based data centers in Luxembourg and Germany face rising energy costs, with electricity prices increasing by 15% annually in Europe (Eurostat, 2023).

2. Carbon Footprint and Regulatory Pressures:

The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) mandates carbon neutrality by 2030 for large enterprises, with penalties of up to 4% of global revenue for non-compliance.

SES's current data centers emit ~50,000 metric tons of CO₂ annually, requiring $2.5M/year in carbon offsets to meet regulatory requirements.

The European Green Deal targets a 55% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030, further tightening regulations on energy-intensive industries.

3. Scalability and Physical Constraints:

Land availability for data center expansion is diminishing, with prime locations in Europe and North America facing zoning restrictions and community opposition.

SES's existing facilities in Betzdorf, Luxembourg, are at 95% capacity, with no viable options for expansion due to local regulatory constraints.

Latency issues persist for remote regions (e.g., Africa, Southeast Asia), where terrestrial data centers struggle to provide sub-100ms latency required for real-time applications.

4. Operational Risks:

Single points of failure: Terrestrial data centers are vulnerable to natural disasters, power outages, and geopolitical risks (e.g., the 2021 Texas power crisis resulted in $1.5B in losses for data center operators).

Security threats: Physical and cybersecurity risks are escalating, with data breaches costing an average of $4.45M per incident (IBM, 2023).

Maintenance costs: Aging infrastructure requires $10M/year in upgrades for SES's existing facilities, with diminishing returns on investment.

SES-Specific Limitations:

Dependency on Terrestrial Infrastructure: SES's current business model relies on ground-based data centers for satellite operations, limiting scalability and innovation.

Missed Market Opportunities: The space-based computing market is projected to grow at 25% CAGR, yet SES lacks a competitive offering in this segment.

Brand Perception: While SES is a leader in satellite communications, it is not perceived as an innovator in data infrastructure, limiting its ability to attract high-value enterprise clients.

Existing facilities operate at 92% capacity with no expansion options in key markets
