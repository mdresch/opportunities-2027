1. Executive Summary

The Local Experience Curator initiative is a strategic project designed to create, package, and promote unique, small-scale local experiences and tours, marketed and sold through platforms such as Eventbrite. This project aligns with the growing demand for authentic, community-driven tourism and experiential travel, which has seen a significant rise in popularity post-pandemic. By leveraging local knowledge and cultural assets, this initiative aims to provide travelers with immersive, off-the-beaten-path experiences while generating economic opportunities for local guides, artisans, and businesses.

The project will operate under the PMBOK 7 framework, emphasizing a principles-based approach to project management. This methodology ensures flexibility, stakeholder engagement, and value delivery, which are critical for the dynamic and creative nature of curating local experiences. The initiative will focus on identifying niche markets, designing compelling experiences, and establishing a scalable model for promotion and sales. Key objectives include achieving a 20% month-over-month growth in bookings within the first six months, maintaining a 90% customer satisfaction rate, and establishing partnerships with at least 15 local vendors by the end of the first year.

The Local Experience Curator project is positioned to capitalize on the shift toward sustainable and responsible tourism, offering travelers meaningful connections with local communities while driving economic benefits for the region. This executive-ready document outlines the project’s objectives, approach, key components, implementation strategy, and metrics for success, providing a comprehensive roadmap for stakeholders.

1.2 Business Need and Value Proposition

Business Need

The global experiential travel market is projected to reach $1.2 trillion by 2027, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 16.5% (Source: Allied Market Research, 2023). Despite this growth, small-scale local experiences often struggle to gain visibility due to fragmented marketing efforts, lack of professional packaging, and limited access to digital sales platforms. 2. Problem Statement

2.1 Current State and Enterprise Limitations

The Local Experience Curator project addresses a critical gap in the experiential travel market: the lack of a centralized, professional platform for curating and promoting small-scale local experiences. Currently, the market operates in a fragmented and inefficient manner, characterized by the following challenges:

1. Fragmented Offerings:

Local experience providers (e.g., guides, artisans, and small businesses) operate independently, with no standardized process for packaging, pricing, or promoting their offerings. This fragmentation leads to inconsistent quality, limited visibility, and missed revenue opportunities. For example, a local artisan may offer a workshop, but without professional marketing or a seamless booking system, they struggle to attract customers beyond their immediate network.

2. Limited Digital Presence:

Many local experience providers lack the technical expertise or resources to leverage digital platforms effectively. While platforms like Eventbrite exist, small-scale providers often underutilize them due to a lack of knowledge about digital marketing, SEO, or platform integration. This results in low online visibility and inefficient sales processes, with bookings often handled manually via email or phone.

3. Inconsistent Customer Experience:

Without a centralized curation process, customers face inconsistent quality and pricing when booking local experiences. For instance, two similar walking tours may vary significantly in content, duration, and cost, leading to customer dissatisfaction and reduced trust in the market. Additionally, the lack of standardized reviews or ratings makes it difficult for customers to identify high-quality experiences.

4. Missed Economic Opportunities:

The current state limits the economic potential of local experience providers. Without a scalable sales model, providers rely on word-of-mouth or local advertising, which restricts their reach and revenue. For example, a local guide offering a historical walking tour may only attract 10-15 customers per month, despite having the capacity to serve 50+ customers with the right marketing and booking infrastructure.

5. Operational Inefficiencies:

The absence of a centralized platform creates operational inefficiencies for both providers and customers. Providers must manage multiple communication channels (e.g., email, phone, social media) to handle inquiries and bookings, leading to delays and errors. Customers, on the other hand, face a cumbersome booking process, often requiring multiple steps to confirm availability, pricing, and payment.

Baseline: As a new project, there is no existing CSAT data.

Target: Achieve a 90% customer satisfaction rate, measured on a 1-5 scale (where 4-5 indicates satisfaction).

Validation Method:

Send post-experience surveys to customers after each booking, asking them to rate their experience.

Monitor online reviews (e.g., Google, TripAdvisor) for qualitative feedback.

Conduct quarterly customer focus groups to gather in-depth insights.

3. Local Partner Engagement

Baseline: The project starts with 0 local partners.

Target: Onboard 15 local partners by the end of Year 1, with 100% active participation (i.e., listing at least one experience on the platform).

Validation Method:

Track partner sign-ups via the platform’s onboarding system.

Monitor active listings and booking rates for each partner.

Conduct quarterly partner satisfaction surveys to assess engagement and address concerns.

4. Revenue Growth

Baseline: The project starts with $0 in revenue.

Target: Generate $1.2 million in gross revenue over the first three years, with the following breakdown:

Year 1: $300,000.

Year 2: $400,000.

Year 3: $500,000.

Validation Method:

Track revenue via Eventbrite’s financial reports and the custom platform’s analytics.

Conduct monthly financial reviews with the Finance Lead to monitor progress and adjust pricing or marketing strategies.

Validation Method:

Conduct time-tracking surveys with local partners before and after platform implementation.

Track repeat bookings via platform analytics and customer accounts.
