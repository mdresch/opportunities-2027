## 1. Overview

This document is the methodology behind the 2027 opportunity pipeline: a repeatable way to identify, evaluate, and execute business opportunities that require minimal to no initial capital, using time, skill, and existing resources instead. The core idea is "sweat equity" — invest effort to build value, monetize it, and reinvest the proceeds into higher-yield opportunities. The actual list of candidate and in-flight ideas lives in [inventory-of-ideas.md](inventory-of-ideas.md); this document is the framework that governs how that list gets built and acted on.

## 2. 2027–2030 Strategic Focus: 17 Pillars & 5 Strategic Assets

The portfolio is structured around **16 Management Consulting Functional Areas**, **Cross-Pillar 2026 Ecosystem Categories**, and a 17th cross-functional pillar (**Intelligence & Autonomous Systems Governance**), covering **all 18 Signature Toolkits** with \$0 initial working capital. Across company sizes, the delivery thesis remains consistent:
- **Small companies** lack the budget or transaction volume to build standard frameworks themselves — package lightweight starter kits, templates, and short advisory sprints they can adopt quickly.
- **Medium and large companies** already run frameworks inconsistently across entities and units — harvest what works, standardize it, and deliver platform- or methodology-backed assessments and governance rollouts.

At the enterprise level, the portfolio converges around **5 Strategic Assets**:

Every opportunity is classified into **Tier A (Flagship)**, **Tier B (Growth)**, **Tier C (Supporting)**, or **Tier D (Thought Leadership)** so pilot execution focuses on the 10–15% of offerings driving 80–90% of enterprise value (see [inventory-of-ideas.md](inventory-of-ideas.md) §17).

## 3. Objectives for 2027

1. **Capital generation** — identify and execute opportunities that generate initial capital without monetary investment.
2. **Scalability** — reinvest initial capital and know-how into higher-yield opportunities, creating a compounding effect.
3. **Resource optimization** — make the best use of available time, skills, and existing resources.
4. **Risk mitigation** — assess and mitigate the risks specific to low-capital ventures.
5. **Continuous improvement** — review and refine the inventory on a regular cadence rather than treating it as fixed.

## 4. Benefits

- **Financial independence** — capital accumulation without relying on external funding.
- **Skill development** — hands-on experience across business domains and company sizes.
- **Flexibility** — ability to pivot quickly when an opportunity underperforms or a better one appears.
- **Sustainability** — a foundation for long-term growth through iterative reinvestment.
- **Compounding optionality** — each engagement produces reusable IP (templates, playbooks, case studies) that makes the next sale easier.

## 5. Requirements, Constraints, Assumptions

**Requirements**
- Access to a computer, internet, and basic tools for research and execution.
- Willingness to learn or adapt skills relevant to a given opportunity.
- A regular time commitment (recommend 10–15 hrs/week) for research, execution, and review.
- A simple system for tracking income, expenses, and capital accumulation per opportunity.

**Constraints**
- No initial monetary capital — opportunities must require $0 to start (small reinvestment from prior wins is fine).
- Limited time due to other commitments.
- Initial skill gaps in some areas, requiring self-education or occasional outsourcing once capital exists.
- Regulatory/legal compliance where relevant (e.g. contracts, tax treatment of side income, advice given as a non-lawyer in the Legal area).

**Assumptions**
- Sufficient market demand exists for the opportunities actually pursued.
- Necessary skills can be acquired within a reasonable timeframe.
- Required tools/resources remain accessible.
- Initial capital and credibility can be reinvested into opportunities with higher growth potential (e.g. small-company engagements building the case studies needed to sell into medium/large companies).

## 6. Process: Research → Pilot → Scale → Review

1. **Research & identification** — scan for low-barrier, zero-capital opportunities within the Section 2 thesis; add every plausible candidate to the inventory, even roughly described.
2. **Evaluation** — for each candidate, fill in estimated capital, estimated time/week, and a feasibility rating (market demand, competition, fit with existing skills).
3. **Pilot selection** — pick the strongest few candidates to actually try, favoring speed-to-signal over completeness of planning. Small-company engagements are generally the fastest path to a first paid pilot.
4. **Pilot execution** — run small, time-boxed pilots; track time spent and any income/output against the idea's entry in the inventory.
5. **Reinvestment** — when a pilot generates capital or a reusable artifact (template, playbook, case study), decide whether to reinvest it into scaling that same opportunity, productizing it for resale, or into a different, higher-yield one.
6. **Review** — revisit the inventory quarterly: promote, scale, park, or kill each idea based on what the pilot data showed.

## 6b. Standard Opportunity Brief (7 Pivotal Questions)

Before promoting any idea to `Pilot`, answer these 7 questions using the 1-sentence positioning formula:

> **Positioning:** For *[Target Buyer & Size]* who struggle with *[Specific Pain]*, **[Idea]** is a *[2026 Category]* backed by *[Signature Toolkit / Existing Asset]* that delivers *[Measurable Outcome]* with \$0 working capital.

1. **Buyer & 2027 Trigger:** Who signs the check, and what 2027 deadline/pressure forces action now?
2. **The Wedge (Pain):** What specific cost, risk, or handoff failure does this eliminate (framed around cost control / risk reduction)?
3. **Deliverable & Existing IP:** What tangible artifact does the client receive, and which existing CBa Consult asset covers ≥70% of it?
4. **Sweat-Equity Feasibility:** Can 1 person deliver Pilot #1 in ≤4–6 weeks at 5–10 hrs/week with \$0 cash outlay?
5. **Outcome & Proof:** What single P&L, time-saved, or compliance KPI proves success and becomes Case Study #1?
6. **Boundaries & Risk:** What is explicitly *out of scope* (e.g., non-legal advice boundary, no over-promised autonomous AI), and what is the #1 failure mode?
7. **Compounding / Scale Path:** After Pilot #1, does this scale into a Medium/Large audit, productized toolkit, recurring retainer, or white-label partner offer?

## 9. Glossary

- **Sweat equity** — value created through time and effort rather than monetary capital.
- **Pilot opportunity** — a small, time-boxed test of an opportunity to check feasibility before committing further.
- **Reinvestment strategy** — the plan for allocating capital (or reusable IP) generated by one opportunity into another, higher-yield one.
- **Feasibility analysis** — an assessment of an opportunity's viability based on market demand, competition, and resource requirements.
- **Harvesting standards** — taking a framework or process that already works well somewhere in a business and driving its consistent application elsewhere in the same organization.
- **Sales-to-operations handoff** — the point where a sold deal becomes delivered work, invoiced revenue, and (where relevant) a signed contract; a frequent source of operational friction at every company size.
- **Spark** — a workspace-stage marker, used in `opportunities/<ID>/Ideation-Spark.md` files, distinct from the `Status` column in [inventory-of-ideas.md](inventory-of-ideas.md). It means a dedicated folder now exists for that opportunity, Tier 1 macro signal check is done, and a first Opportunity Brief draft (§6b) exists — but Tier 2 deeper research and the Tier 3 pre-pilot gate are still open. An idea stays `Idea` in the inventory's `Status` column throughout the Spark stage; only a real pilot moves it to `Pilot`.
